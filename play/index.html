playground · a room with a door

A room with a door sits in a billiard table. Tens of thousands of non-interacting point particles (f32 on the GPU, specular walls, one speed) start packed inside and leak out. A system/environment partition is just a closed surface you count across: when it lines up with the room's walls the count relaxes cleanly and irreversibly (effusion); drawn anywhere else it recrosses noisily and gives no arrow. Exploratory playground — no claims, nothing pre-registered.

Try: shrink the door to 0 (nothing flows, the clock never starts) · remove the walls (recrossing, and on this plain rectangle the gas keeps long memories) · switch to the circle and drag it off the walls · add the scatterer (Sinai: the outer table turns chaotic and mixes) · press reverse — every velocity negates and the whole history retraces, up to f32 rounding. Watch Nin fold back and the clock unwind: the arrow was in the initial condition, not in the dynamics.

Assumptions
  • 2D billiards in the unit square: continuous f32 positions, no lattice. Free flight speed 0.02 table widths per step; one speed for all particles; specular, infinitely thin walls.
  • N non-interacting point particles; initial condition drawn by Philox4x32-10 keyed (seed, step, site) — the seed is in the URL. Crossings are counted once per step, from which side of the surface a particle starts and ends the step on.
  • Reverse negates every velocity; specular reflection and the wall snap are time-symmetric, so the run retraces its own history up to f32 rounding.
  • The rough curve is leak-only effusion, τ = π·A_room/(door·⟨v⟩): return flux through the door, imperfect mixing in the outer rectangle and the scatterer all shift the real relaxation.
  • On the plain rectangle every reflection flips exactly one velocity component, so |vx| and |vy| are conserved: the outer gas spreads in columns, mixes poorly, and the count stays above the area-fraction line for a long time. The scatterer breaks both integrals (Sinai billiard) and the approach to the area fraction is roughly twice as fast — but the small chamber between door and table wall always returns particles quickly, so both relax more slowly than the rough curve.
  • What would kill the intuition shown here: wall tunnelling (watch N_in with the door shut), speed drift, or aligned counts that stop descending.
